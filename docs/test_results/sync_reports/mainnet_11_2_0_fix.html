Mainnet Sync Report - 11.2.0 Fix

Mainnet sync from genesis, comparing cardano-node 11.2.0 with an experimental build containing memory-regression fixes at revision 45aa403634fe2bb8817e65e7edf6b1d639a3bada. This report labels that build 11.2.0-fix; the label is not a separate release version.

The 11.2.0 report found higher retained memory and slower syncing than 11.1.1, with configuration differences that limited attribution. Here, both runs use matching effective startup settings, including Genesis mode and the same snapshot policy. The comparison checks whether the experimental build improves those measurements without changing those settings.

Summary

  • Memory drops substantially. Peak RSS falls from 22.08 to 16.75 GiB (-24.1%), and peak garbage-collector live data falls from 18.03 to 10.93 GiB (-39.4%). The peak RSS reduction remains over matching slot ranges.

  • Most of the sync slowdown disappears. Reported sync time falls from 132,111 to 110,485 seconds (-16.4%). Babbage takes 28.6% less time over the same chain range and accounts for 81.6% of the saving.

  • Utilization improves without increasing total CPU work. Mean CPU load rises from 112% to 123% of one core, while total CPU time falls 8.0%.

  • Snapshot behavior changes despite matching settings. Completed snapshot writes increase from nine to 176, and major collections increase from 812 to 1,321. Total allocation remains approximately 257 TB.

  • A residual historical gap remains. Peak RSS is still 8.6% above the 11.0.1 baseline. Early-chain memory use is essentially unchanged from 11.2.0. Those historical comparisons are not configuration-controlled.

The experimental build finishes approximately six hours earlier while syncing 512,810 more slots (+0.25%). Babbage alone saves 17,638 seconds, or 4 h 54 min, over the same fixed range of 61.344 million slots. This reverses most of the Babbage slowdown observed in the preceding report.

Conway covers slightly more chain in the experimental run: its recorded length increases from 68.256 to 68.688 million slots. Recorded throughput rises from 1,707 to 1,885 slots/s (+10.4%).

Memory Consumption

Peak RSS falls by 5.33 GiB and mean RSS by 3.24 GiB. The RSS curves track closely through the early chain, then separate around slots 55-60 million. The experimental build holds roughly 15.4 GiB through much of Babbage, then reaches a stable 16.75 GiB late-chain plateau. It never exceeds 20 GiB.

Peak live data falls by 7.09 GiB (-39.4%), and mean live data falls 20.3%. This shows that the improvement includes less retained live data, not just reduced unused heap capacity. Allocation changes by only +0.2%, while the major-GC count rises 62.7%.

The absence of heap decreases in the experimental run is not, by itself, evidence of a leak. The allocated heap reaches a lower plateau even though major collections continue. The run does not establish behavior over longer periods after reaching the chain tip.

The observed snapshot schedule nevertheless changes substantially. The logs contain nine completed snapshot writes in 11.2.0 and 176 in the experimental run. The original run’s first snapshot completes more than five hours after the first chain extension; the experimental run’s first completes in less than one minute, followed by writes approximately every 10 minutes.

The startup delay settings therefore should not be treated as a description of identical runtime scheduling in the two implementations. The changed snapshot behavior accompanies lower retained data and a higher major-GC count, but this comparison does not isolate which individual implementation change produces each improvement.

Matching effective settings remove the principal configuration confounder from the preceding report. They do not control peer availability, storage conditions, or every runtime detail. This is one run per build, not a repeated benchmark or a demonstration that every part of the historical memory gap is resolved.

Methodology

RSS, RTS heap, and CPU statistics come from the result JSON files. Memory statistics exclude zero-valued fields, which represent missing resource samples, and require a recorded tip to match the graph script. This yields 130,567 and 108,891 usable memory samples, respectively.

Whole-run means and threshold fractions are sample-weighted, not normalized to equal time spent at each slot. Different sync speeds can change those statistics. The matching-slot checks compare peaks without that weighting issue.

CPU means retain genuine zero-load samples and use stored values without rescaling. The smoothed figure averages samples into one million-slot bands, keeping bands with at least 20 samples. Total CPU time is the change in the node log’s cumulative CPU-tick counter divided by 100.

GC live bytes, major collections, allocation, and completed snapshot writes come from the full node logs. They contain 130,576 and 108,897 resource samples, including samples without a recorded tip. Live-data means are arithmetic means of those samples. Major-GC and allocation totals are the last counter minus the first counter in each resource-log window, not process-lifetime maxima. Allocation uses decimal TB.

Heap decreases count successive decreases among the nonzero, tip-associated heap samples in timestamp order. Threshold fractions refer to heap, not RSS. The timing-boundary cross-check uses the first logged chain extension at slot 0 and the recorded sync-completion timestamp. Per-epoch timings remain based on observed epochs; the experimental JSON’s epoch-duration series begins at epoch 4 even though its era summary correctly counts Byron from epoch 0.
